Several important computer systems in America failed within a few days. Microsoft services stopped working, air-traffic control had communication problems, several banks had technical trouble, and the National Security Agency lost part of its classified communication system.

Many Americans wondered whether this was an enemy attack. Officials gave different explanations.

The air-traffic problem was linked to a faulty new router. Microsoft's outage came from a software patch with a hidden bug.

The problems at some banks and the NSA were also connected to software updates. There is little evidence that hackers or uncontrolled AI caused the incidents.

However, AI may still be part of the story. AI tools can find weaknesses in computer systems much faster than people could before.

This creates pressure on companies and government agencies to repair many problems at the same time. Anthropic's Mythos model is very good at finding and using software weaknesses.

Selected organisations use it to check their own code before criminals can attack it. This is useful, but the number and speed of repairs can overwhelm technical teams and old systems.

In the short term, Americans may see more outages and become more afraid of cyber-attacks. In the long term, the repairs should remove many weaknesses and make computer systems safer.

The main idea is that efforts to improve security can create new risks while the work is being done.
