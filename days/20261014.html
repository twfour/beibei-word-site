03 ROADMAP / 内容简介

文章借 2026 年诺贝尔经济学奖回顾宏观经济学如何从凯恩斯主义与货币主义之争，发展到以个体行为为基础的新凯恩斯框架。

奖项授予 Robert Hall 和 Michael Woodford：Hall 研究消费者如何根据对终身收入的预期平滑消费，Woodford 则用自然利率和纯信用经济模型解释央行如何通过利率稳定通胀与就业。

文章指出，这两人的共同贡献是为宏观经济学建立“微观基础”：不再只从货币总量、通胀等统计数字自上而下建模，而是从消费者和企业的选择推导整体经济结果。

Hall 的模型认为，理性消费者只会在获得有关终身收入的新信息时改变支出，因此个人消费轨迹像醉汉回家，短期随机偏移但大方向稳定；Woodford 的框架则认为，市场利率低于自然利率会引发通胀繁荣，高于自然利率则会造成通缩，央行应清晰沟通反应规则。

后半部分集中讨论理论局限：自然利率无法直接观察且会快速变化，现实中的消费者常把临时收入花掉，并不像模型假设的那样完全理性；模型也曾忽略金融部门，难以预见金融危机。

如今前瞻指引可能造成市场与央行互相揣测的“镜厅”，疫情、战争等供给冲击和高债务又迫使央行在通胀与失业之间作出痛苦取舍。

文章最终强调，获奖理论重塑了央行思维，但并未给现实政策提供简单答案。

04 EASIER ENGLISH / 简明改写

The article is about the 2026 Nobel Prize in Economics. It went to Robert Hall and Michael Woodford, two important New Keynesian economists.

Their work helped explain how the choices of individual people and businesses can shape the whole economy. Earlier economists often studied large totals, such as the money supply, inflation and unemployment.

Hall and Woodford helped build macroeconomics on “microfoundations”. This means starting with the behaviour of consumers and companies and then using it to understand national economic results.

Hall studied consumer spending. His model says people think about their future income and try to keep their spending fairly smooth over their lives.

A temporary payment should not greatly change their spending unless it changes what they expect to earn in the long term. Woodford studied interest rates.

His model says central banks should compare market rates with a natural rate that keeps the economy balanced. The article also explains the weaknesses of these ideas.

The natural interest rate cannot be seen directly and can change quickly. Real consumers are not always rational and may spend temporary payments immediately.

Older versions of the models also paid too little attention to banks and financial crises. Today, central banks face pandemics, wars, supply problems and high government debt.

They must choose how much inflation or unemployment they are willing to accept. The main message is that Hall and Woodford changed economic thinking, but their models cannot remove the difficult choices that governments and central banks must make.
